    01 · The problem

    The page updates. The old figure goes.

    Fuel prices, fertiliser sales, mandi rates, forex reserves: the numbers India argues over live on pages that are updated in place. Revising a provisional figure is normal and often right. But once the old figure is gone, nobody outside the publisher can show what was official on a given day, or prove whether a number was changed at all.

    “What did the page say on 1 May?”Usually unanswerable.
    “Was last week's figure changed?”Usually a matter of opinion.
    “Is this archive itself honest?”Here, something you can check.

    02 · The chain

    Each week sealed to the one before.

    Every snapshot is stored as a record carrying the SHA-256 fingerprint of the record before it. Change one figure in one old week and every fingerprint after it stops matching. The newest fingerprint, the head, is then published somewhere the archive keeper doesn't control.

    1Fetch

    Politely, and fingerprinted first.

    requestsone at a time
    identified asKanakku/0.1
    robots.txtrespected

    The raw bytes are hashed before anything reads them, so the archive can always show exactly what was served.

    2Read

    Into a table that always encodes the same way.

    numbersintegers only
    keys, rowssorted
    fingerprinttable_sha256

    No floating point anywhere, so the same table is the same bytes on every machine, in Python and in this browser.

    3Seal

    Each record holds the last one's hash.

    seq 57–
    seq 58 prev–
    seq 58–

    These are the real last two records, hashed in your browser. The middle line has to equal the first, or the chain is broken.

    4Witness

    Publish the head somewhere else.

    published head–
    matches–

    A chain can't see its own end being cut off, or a keeper rewriting all of it and re-sealing every record. A head kept somewhere else can.

    03 · Tamper lab

    Go on. Rewrite history.

    This is the real archive, all 59 records, loaded into your browser. Pick an attack. The page forges a copy and runs the same checks as the Python verifier: once with the chain alone, once against the published head.

    Nothing here touches the published file. Each attack works on a copy in memory and is discarded.

    04 · Revision or rounding?

    One line of maths, 143 false alarms gone.

    The RBI prints each week's level and its change from last week. Level minus change should equal last week's level. It often misses by one, because each figure was rounded on its own. Round n figures and combine them: rounding alone can move the result by at most n/2, and since the result is a whole number, by at most ⌊n/2⌋. Anything bigger can't be rounding.

    05 · A year of reserves

    59 Fridays. Not one restated.

    Every release of the RBI's Weekly Statistical Supplement table Foreign Exchange Reserves from 5 September 2025 to 16 October 2026. Move along the line: each week shows what was first published, what the next release implies it was, and the rupee rate the table itself implies.

    06 · When the base moves

    Which growth figure was official when?

    Not every series is as steady. In February 2026 India moved its national accounts to a 2022–23 base. Kanakku records that as a schema change, then the revisions on either side of it. Each figure was, for a while, the official one.

    08 · Where it stops

    What it proves, and what it can't.

    1. It proves what it saw, not what others were shown. Only a publisher signing its own releases could prove that.
    2. The year was read on one day. Pages were collected on 23 October 2026, so an edit made before then shows up only through the implied-level checks. Weekly collection from now on closes that gap.
    3. The witness is only as strong as where the head is published. Here: a heads file and the git history. Independent witnesses would be stronger.
    4. Two GDP figures come from news reports of the official releases, and are marked as such.
    5. The figures belong to the Reserve Bank of India, reproduced with acknowledgement for research. Weekly collection waits for the Bank's reply to a request for permission.
    6. This page keeps nothing about you. No cookies, no analytics, no third-party requests, no forms. Everything above ran in your browser, on files from this site.
